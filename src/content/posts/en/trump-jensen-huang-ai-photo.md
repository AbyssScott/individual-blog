I saw this AI-generated image that Mr. Trump posted on [Truth Social](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117666713950631493) to celebrate Jensen Huang's return as NVIDIA's CEO. I found it pretty interesting, so I decided to share it here.

![AI-generated image shared by Trump on Truth Social celebrating Jensen Huang's return to NVIDIA](/individual-blog/images/trump-huang-ai-celebration.webp)

I think this picture tells us at least a few things.

First, Mr. Trump really likes the original photograph that inspired this image—the one taken after the assassination attempt at his campaign rally in Pennsylvania in 2024, showing him with blood on his face, raising his fist in the air beneath the American flag, surrounded by Secret Service agents. To be fair, I really like that [photograph](https://www.newyorker.com/news/daily-comment/the-attempt-on-donald-trumps-life-and-an-image-that-will-last) too. The composition and visual impact are both quite remarkable.

Second, Mr. Trump and Jensen Huang really do seem to get along. They've already been [calling each other and exchanging compliments](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/14/trump-jensen-huang-nvidia-ai-all-in-summit). Now Trump is even happy to lend Huang his iconic pose, letting him have a turn raising his fist in triumph. There's also something that might qualify as a bit of dry humor here: the image borrows Trump's classic meme, while the computer used to generate this AI meme might very well have been running on one of Huang's GPUs. Well, I suppose that's one way of helping each other out.

Third, this is hardly the first time Mr. Trump has used AI-generated images or videos for publicity, and he seems to enjoy doing so quite a lot. As far as I remember, one of his most outrageous experiments with this was probably the [video depicting Barack and Michelle Obama as apes](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-shares-racist-video-depicting-obamas-apes-2026-02-06/), posted by his account in February 2026. The White House later deleted it. Apparently, even the White House thought Mr. Trump had taken things a little too far that time.

In the past, if you wanted a photograph that served your publicity needs, you might have had to wait for just the right moment or spend time editing one in Photoshop. Now, with AI, you can simply generate whatever image you want. I suppose this is one of the more interesting new forms of publicity to emerge in the 2020s, and there's already some interesting political science research on the subject. Perhaps I'll write something about it another time. For now, though, we can at least see that Trump doesn't reserve his enthusiasm for AI-generated content exclusively for political issues. He's rather like a kid who has discovered a new toy and can't put it down.

Fourth, Mr. Trump apparently thinks Alice Huang should own a piece of red lingerie.

Of course, I suspect this AI image also carries a message beyond what we see in it. For example, I imagine Trump might also be trying to say something like this: I'm delighted that Jensen Huang is finally back at NVIDIA, but don't you forget that none of this would have happened without Mr. Trump and his administration doing some serious lobbying of NVIDIA's board.

Let's look at what happened over the past few days. On November 17, NVIDIA's board chairman was still saying quite clearly during the company's [earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nvda/transcripts/739383-q3-2027/):

> *The NVIDIA board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.*

Just six days later, however, NVIDIA's board announced in an [official statement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Board-Reinstates-Jensen-Huang-as-President-CEO-and-Board-Director/default.aspx):

> *The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA...*

...WTF? **A complete 180-degree turnaround in just six days?** I suspect something must have happened during those six days.

My guess is that the US government played a fairly significant role in this change of heart. According to NVIDIA's [announcement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Board-Reinstates-Jensen-Huang-as-President-CEO-and-Board-Director/default.aspx), the board *“received input from senior levels of the United States government”* before making its decision, and concluded that Huang's return *“poses no national security risk.”*

*The Economist* also reported in [this article](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2026/11/24/republican-lawmakers-are-in-revolt-over-jensen-huangs-return) that President Trump and Vice-President J. D. Vance worked together to secure Huang's reinstatement. Trump publicly stated on social media that the US government had no reason to believe Huang's return to NVIDIA would pose a national security risk, and that it had no evidence of unlawful conduct on his part. In an interview with CNBC, Trump also acknowledged that he had communicated with NVIDIA's board, directly or indirectly, and even said: *“And I convinced them, I made the deal.”*

Well, it seems President Trump really does "love" Jensen Huang.

I honestly can't remember the last time Trump went to such lengths for someone, even getting personally involved and putting so much effort into helping them out.

So I suspect that, besides celebrating Huang's return, Trump might also be using this AI image to celebrate his own accomplishment. After all, having gone to so much trouble and finally gotten the job done, wouldn't he want everyone to know just how much he contributed?

As we might say in Chinese: **“功成必定有我” — “If there's a success, you can be sure I had a hand in it!”**

I can understand how Mr. Trump feels. After all, success isn't exactly an everyday occurrence for him. Back when I was in middle and high school, whenever I managed to do well on an exam for once, I wanted my whole family to know about it.

I came across a new paper in *Political Analysis* just before going to bed: [An Image Is Worth K Topics: A Visual Structural Topic Model with Pretrained Image Embeddings](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2026.10062).

![First page of the paper](/individual-blog/images/paper-first-page.png)

This looks pretty cool!

One thing I've noticed is that, with the development of [Text as Data](https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mps028) and related methods such as [structural topic models](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12103), social scientists are no longer limiting themselves to analyzing text. They are increasingly finding ways to turn other forms of information about human behavior and attitudes into data for empirical research. These include people's facial expressions ([Boussalis et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055421000666)), voices ([Dietrich et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055419000467); [Mestre & Ryan, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2025.10031)), and physical appearance ([Jeon et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2026.10038)), as well as forms of media such as images ([Torres, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2023.32)) and videos ([Lu & Pan, 2022](https://doi.org/10.5117/CCR2022.2.002.LU); [Hu & Fu, 2026](https://doi.org/10.5117/CCR2026.4.4.HU)), and multimodal social media content ([Chen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2025.10010)). This paper is about **Image as Data**.

I think this is a great sign! This is exactly the sort of direction I'd like to see social science methodology move in.

But there's one thing about this paper that puzzles me: **Why didn't the authors include an approach that uses a multimodal large language model directly for visual topic analysis in their comparison?**

My intuition is that today's leading general-purpose AI models might already be better than traditional topic models at understanding images and identifying their underlying themes. I'm not so sure about images, but in text analysis, there is already [evidence](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2025.10025) that using LLMs directly can outperform traditional machine learning methods on certain tasks.

Given the vast amounts of image and text data that today's multimodal models have been trained on, my guess is that they could handle the entire process on their own, from image understanding to topic discovery and coding, and do a pretty good job of it.

Please correct me if I'm wrong. I'm something of an amateur in this area, and I'm mostly going on intuition here.
